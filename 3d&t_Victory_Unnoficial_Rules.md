# Creating characters

## Attributes/Atributos (page 38)

These numbers are the basic traits of the character, the measure of what he can do. The higher, the better.

Attributes range from 0 to 5. One character point buys 1 attribute point.

- **0** below average - You are weak, lacking hatred! And spend a few more points.
- **1** average human - You are average. In this game, it is practically an insult.
- **2** above average - Nothing very exciting, but you won't embarrass yourself.
- **3** well above average - Nothing very exciting, but you won't embarrass yourself.
- **4** peak human - Good! Everyone trusts you (and maybe they are taking advantage of that).
- **5** superhuman - Great! Few are better suited than you in this subject. Fantastic! You are the best at what you do!

These numbers are the basic traits of the character, the measure of what he can do. The higher the better.

You can’t have any attribute above 5 when you create your character, even if you have points to spend. But you can exceed this limit later, with experience.

When something (like a advantage) temporarily increases an attribute, it can exceed 5. It will then return to normal.

You can have one or more attributes at 0, if you want a character who is very weak in a certain area, but you should have at least 1 point in each attribute. There are only three, don’t be stingy! There are no attributes that are just physical, or just mental, or just social. Each of the three, in some way, affects both physical/material actions and mental/emotional actions.

The attributes are **Power**, **Hability**, and **Resistance**. Also abbreviated as **P**, **H**, and **R**.

**Power** is the ability to impose your effort, your presence, to perform an action. It is your physical ability to lift weights, push and pull things, cause damage with punches, kicks and shots. It is also your strength of personality, personal magnetism, sociability and ability to convince or charm people. You need Power to overcome monsters, villains, locked doors, annoying security guards, grumpy teachers and other obstacles that insist on getting in your way. Power is strength and charisma. Power also tells you how much effort you can put into making a heroic effort. Power defines your Action Points.

**Hability** is the ability to think and act. To do things, solve problems, perceive your surroundings, understand the world. It represents agility, balance and motor coordination. It is also your intelligence, quick reasoning and ability to deduce. You need Hability for daring things like leaping over buildings, climbing mountains, or grabbing helicopters in midair, and also for feats of mental competence like solving mysteries and winning strategy contests. Hability is dexterity and intelligence. It is also the Hability that allows you to perform special, extraordinary actions. Hability defines your Mana Points.

**Resistance** tells you how much punishment you can withstand before you fall or give in. It serves to resist and avoid being affected by other characters or effects. It represents your vitality, health, and physical constitution — but also your conviction, willpower, and mental resilience, to avoid being dominated or tricked. When receiving an attack, a high Resistance helps reduce injuries and other harmful effects. Resistance is vigor and willpower. Resistance tells you how much damage you tolerate before being defeated. Resistance defines your Health Points.

## Resources
Resources are a type of secondary attribute. They are points that you can spend to activate effects, perform incredible feats, or simply stay alive. Each resource is derived from an attribute. Multiply the attribute x5 (or x1, for Action Points) to find out how many points of that resource you have.

**Action Points** are like adrenaline rushes or bursts of luck. They are used to improve tests and, using certain optional rules, various other benefits. Action Points are derived from Power. So, AP = P.

**Mana Points** are your fuel for fantastic abilities that require inner energy, such as advantages and techniques. Mana Points are derived from Skill. So, MP = Hx5

**Health Points** are, as the name suggests, the "life bar". A character who has lost HP may be injured, bruised, upset, bleeding profusely, shaken in his conviction, but he can still act and fight. Health Points are derived from Stamina. HP = Rx5

A 0 attribute is 1 resource point. If you decided to have H0 (seriously?), you only have 1 Mana Point. If anything reduces damage or MP expenditure by half, round up.

## Skills (page 40)

A skill is a set of abilities and knowledge that a character has, because he/she has trained or learned (or received in some fantastic way). Fighting, driving vehicles, researching and learning about subjects, playing sports... Everything can be a skill.
**Each skill costs 1 point.** When you acquire one, you will be an expert, a great connoisseur in that area. If you chose Machines, for example, then you know everything about driving, piloting, operating, building and repairing any machine, device or vehicle. If you chose Animals, you know everything about how to care for, train, treat and tame any type of animal. And so on. After attributes, skills are the most important thing in 3D&TVictory.**Whenever you make a test related to a skill you have, you roll an extra die.** Every character __should__ have at least one skill! Preferably, more than one.

Below are the skills and some possibilities they offer, but you can imagine others. If a situation does not appear here, simply choose the skill that best fits the problem. And the game continues.

- **Animals** You know how to care for, train, ride, and deal with animals and other irrational creatures, such as various monsters. You can replace Medicine (only for animals). With good results on the tests, you can even communicate with animals.
- **Art** You know how to perform artistic skills such as singing, dancing, playing music, cooking, cosplaying (and disguising yourself), drawing, appraising art objects, and others.
- **Athletics** You know the many types of sports and their rules, and you are skilled in physical activities such as running, climbing, swimming, acrobatics, balancing, jumping, and others. Influence. You know how to convince others to believe in something or do what you want. This involves things like diplomacy, leadership, intimidation, seduction, bluffing, hypnosis, smooth talking, bargaining, obtaining information, and others.
- **Fighting** You know how to attack and defend yourself in combat, whether hand-to-hand or at a distance. It also involves the theoretical part of combat, such as recognizing fighting styles, analyzing enemy techniques, and developing tactics or action plans — including confrontations between large groups, such as armies. Violent conflicts (almost) always require Fighting tests!
- **Streetwise** You know how to do tricky or illegal things, such as building (and sabotaging) traps, breaking doors and locks, pickpocketing, creating (and deciphering) encrypted messages, disguising yourself, forging objects, being stealthy, intimidating, tracking clues and footprints…
- **Machines** You know how to operate, build and repair machines, vehicles and devices of all kinds. You also know how to deal with computers, hack systems, search the internet, play games or act in simulations and virtual realities. You can replace Medicine (only for constructs).
- **Medicine** You know how to perform first aid, diagnoses, treat diseases and poisons, perform surgeries and all kinds of health knowledge. You can wake an unconscious character or stabilize a nearly dead character.
- **Mystic** You know about supernatural forces and mystical arts. When attacking or defending yourself with magical or supernatural powers (such as the Magic advantage), use this skill. It is also used to recognize, counterattack, and theorize about occult knowledge, magic, and magical creatures.
- **Perception** You know how to use your senses to better perceive the world around you. Used to hear very quiet noises, notice distant, hidden, or disguised things and characters, read lips, track clues, avoid being surprised, and even as a “sixth sense” to try to notice if someone is lying or acting suspiciously. Knowledge. You know everything about everything, any theoretical knowledge in science, languages, and even supernatural subjects, or how and where to research them. Note that this is a broad skill, encompassing all fields, suitable for those exaggerated scientists or super nerds who know everything!
- **Survival** You know how to survive and navigate adverse conditions. You can find and produce food, build shelters, track tracks, recognize wild creatures, build and sabotage traps, be stealthy, swim, predict the weather, and more.

If these skills seem too exaggerated to you, that’s because they are! In 3DeT Victory, as in anime, a character is almost never “average” at something they’ve trained for — they’re either incredibly good, an artistic genius, an extraordinary talent. Or they’re completely incompetent, but that’s another disadvantage.

To be average at something, just don’t choose the skill. A hero who works as a taxi driver doesn’t need the Machines skill in his daily life — driving in normal traffic is a simple task, and doesn’t even require tests. Even a racing driver can get by without the skill, with a high Skill and advantages that bring bonuses (like Agile) or Gain. Having the skill, however, puts you among the best in the world. If you also have Mastery, you might even be the best in the world!

Note that some skills cover similar areas: a character with Art or Lore knows pop culture, while another character might know how to track because he has Streetwise, Perception, or Survival. In game terms, none of this makes any difference—a character who knows how to fish might do it as a hobby or to survive. Negotiate with the GM whether a skill can be used for that test.

## Advantages (page 42)
In addition to attributes and skills, you can use your points to buy advantages. These are special abilities, techniques, and powers that the hero has trained, received, or is simply born with. Each advantage offers a different benefit.
As with everything else in this game, the explanation for the origin of the advantages is up to you! Your Reach could be due to a bow and arrows, eye beams, a powerful voice, or arms that stretch. You could be Charismatic because you are a cute little animal, an inspiring leader, or a pop star. Every advantage has an effect, but you are free to invent the cause.

Also note that when an advantage mentions attack and defense, this doesn’t just apply to fighting. A Special Attack is useful for punching someone in the face, but also for winning an argument or winning over your crush. There is no limit to how many advantages a character can have, if they can afford them. 

### Acceleration (1pt)
You move very fast. You can spend 1MP to make an extra move on your turn. You can also spend 1MP before rolling the dice to receive a Gain in initiative, or in Skill tests to run, flee or chase an opponent. 

### +Action (1pt each)
You have more Action Points, in addition to those offered by your Power. Each time you buy this advantage you receive +2AP. So, if you have P3 (3AP) and pay 1 point for +Action, you now have 5AP. You can buy this advantage multiple times, for new increases of +2AP.

### Hoarder (1pt)
You collect junk at home. Lie. The more your attacks hit, the harder you hit or argue. Whenever you hit with an attack (i.e., when you cause damage), you can spend 2MP to gain +1 Power on your next attack. If you hit again, you can spend 2MP again for P+2. And so on, up to a maximum of P+5 (always 2MP per attack). The attacks can be against the same target or different targets. If you miss an attack, or for some reason fail to attack (doing anything that does not cause damage), the bonuses end.

### Agile (1pt)
You have great agility and motor coordination. You receive H+2 on tests involving agility, coordination or balance, including initiative tests. This bonus is included on critical hits. You can also spend 2MP before making one of these tests to achieve a critical hit on a 5 or 6.

### Helper (1pt each)
You have something or someone you can count on, whether in combat or other situations. The helper can be a person — an apprentice, a mercenary, a cleric, a nurse, a mechanic… Or an animal — a pet, a hunting animal, a mount. Or a monster, an alien, a robot, a vehicle, an enchanted object… Anything that matches the abilities it offers. Thus, a Fighter helper can be an intelligent magical sword that appears in your hands, a fairy in love who cheers for your victory, or a mysterious vigilante who provides help and leaves saying “my work here is done”. Once per round, you can spend 2MP to summon the helper. The type of help depends on its function:
- Healer. Heals 2D HP to you or another nearby character, or allows you to repeat a Resistance test against some negative effect (paralysis, poison, disease, sadness…) after having failed.
- Specialist. Choose a skill. Gained on a test with this skill, except to defend or attack.
- Familiar. This supernatural helper costs 1MP to be summoned (instead of 2) and halves the MP cost to use another advantage in the round. • Fighter. Gained on an attack test, or on defense tests for one round.
- Mount. Grants an extra move, or a Gain on an initiative test or to run or chase an opponent. Costs 1MP. You can buy this advantage multiple times to have different helpers (or the same helper with different functions). However, in both cases, you can only receive one help per round.

### Range (1-2pt)
Your attacks hit further away. This applies to all types of attacks, such as shooting, throwing things, stretching, shouting louder... 1pt You can hit enemies that are Far Away without penalty, and Very Far Away with a Miss. 2pt You can also hit Very Far Away without penalty. Range also affects your other advantages. You can use your Healing on distant allies, or Magic on distant targets. Without Range, unless an advantage says otherwise, you can only use it against targets that are Nearby.

### Nullification (2pt)
You can prevent the target from using one of its powers. After using an advantage in your presence (Far or less), you can try to use an action and 3MP to try to nullify that advantage. The target makes an R roll (9 + Power of the nullifier) ​​to try to avoid it. If it fails, it can no longer use that advantage until the end of the scene, cancel the effect, or be defeated. You can also nullify an advantage if you know in advance that the target has it. You may have already seen him use it, or found out because he is Telepathic, or already know it because he is Famous or Infamous, or with a skill roll (Lore, Streetwise, Perception, 12). Each turn after the first, the target can make an R test (9 + Power of the nullifier) ​​to cancel the effect. Only one advantage can be nullified at a time. If you want to nullify another advantage, the first one will be released.

### Arena (1pt each)
You do best in a certain type of terrain. When you are in that place, you can spend 2MP to have Gain in a test. The Arena is chosen by the player, but always with the approval of the GM. Some suggestions are:
- Water (beach, boats, rain, water surface, underwater)
- Sky (in tests against opponents, only counts if he is also flying)
- Cities (streets, rooftops, top of buildings, rooms, hallways, stairs)
- Wilderness (deserts, forests, mountains, swamps, glaciers…)
- Underground (caves, vaults, sewers)
- A category of specific places (bars, hospitals, libraries, shopping malls…)
- A single place in the world (probably your base of operations) and neighborhoods.

### Artifact (1pt or more)
You have a special, unique item. Only you can use it. It can be a magic sword, an experimental ray gun, or a robotic armor, your choice. Unlike some common equipment, which does not affect the rules, your artifact offers game benefits. Each point spent on this advantage is worth 10XP to purchase a weapon, armor, or accessory (but not a vehicle) as described in Rewards.

### Special Attack (1pt each)
When making an attack, you can spend energy to activate a superior weapon or technique. Each effect has its cost in MP:

- Area. All characters Near the target also receive the attack. 3MP.
- Shock. Reinforcing the attack with your resolve, you attack using Resistance instead of Power. Any bonus or penalty that would affect your Power now affects Resistance. 1MP.
- Far. You can hit one step further away. This attack is cumulative with the Range advantage (it can hit even Out of Range!). 1MP.
- Spiritual. The target loses MP instead of HP with this attack. 1MP.
- Charge. When attacking a target Far or further away, you also move to the target with the same action. You do not use a movement, but you must be able to reach that target with a movement. 1MP.
- Multiple. You hit more targets within range. 1MP per extra target, up to a maximum equal to the Skill. Each target makes a separate defense test, but you use the same attack test for all. This only applies to multiple targets: to attack the same target multiple times, use Powerful to represent the damage of the extra attacks.
- Piercing. The target has Defense Loss. 2MP.
- Dangerous. The attack causes a critical hit on a 5 or 6. 1MP.
- Powerful. When you score a critical hit on the attack, you can spend 2MP to add the Power once more.
- Potent. Increases the Power of the attack by P+2. You can buy this effect multiple times (for example, 3pt for a Special Attack that spends 3MP and attacks with P+6). 1MP for each P+2.
- Accurate. If you calculate well, you attack using Skill instead of Power. Any bonus or penalty that would affect your Power now affects your Skill. 1MP.
- Titanic. You surpass your limits and your potential! You have an automatic critical hit on your attack. 3MP.

You can buy this advantage more times to have multiple effects. You can choose, at the time of the attack, which effects you want to use or not. A Penetrating and Perilous Attack, for example, costs 4MP.

For effects that reduce the MP cost, a Special Attack with multiple effects is considered a single advantage.

### Base (1pt)
You have a base of operations, headquarters, hideout, orbital station, apartment, room in your parents' house, or other safe haven where you can rest. Or where you can escape.
Through teleportation (or another means you invent), you can reach your base in any situation outside of combat. At the GM's discretion, risky or dangerous situations (such as being caught in a trap) prevent you to reach the base. You can take with you a maximum number of allies equal to your Power + Skill. Note that this is a one-way trip — you can reach your base from anywhere, but you cannot return to where you were! To do so, you will have to resort to other means. Your base is considered the ideal place to rest and recover resources. In addition, all your tests performed there have Gain.

### Brutal (1pt each)
When attacking enemies, in addition to causing damage, you recover your own energy.

- Life. For every 3 points of damage caused to an opponent in combat, you recover 1 Life Point.
- Mana. For every 3 points of damage caused to an opponent in combat, you recover 1 Mana Point.
- Defeat. Each time you defeat an opponent in combat, you recover 3 Life Points and 1 Mana Point.

You can buy each version for 1pt each, and/or the same version multiple times. Their effects stack.
This advantage only works in real combat, against real opponents. You do not recover health or mana by attacking walls, nor do you recover willing allies with Healing or Regeneration!

### Charismatic (1pt)
You have an easy time making people like you. You receive P+2 on social tests or tests that involve interactions with other people. This bonus is included in critical hits. You can also spend 2MP before making one of these tests to get a critical hit on a 5 or 6.

### Clone (1pt)
You can create exact copies of yourself, spending 2MP per clone and one movement. Whenever you receive a successful attack, you suffer no damage, but a copy disappears. Remaining copies last until the end of the scene. The clones do whatever you want, but do not perform their own actions.
They can help with certain tasks at the GM's discretion (for example, distracting opponents), generating Gain for tests.
An attacker with an adequate Sense can make a Perception test (9 + Power of the copyist) to ignore your clones and attack you normally.

### Confusion (1pt)
You are able to make opponents dizzy.
Make an attack and spend 2MP. If you beat the target's defense, in addition to suffering
damage, the target becomes confused. In this state, the target cannot choose the targets of its
skills or advantages, including itself: the targets are chosen at random by the GM. The confusion lasts until the end of the scene, or until the target takes damage. Each turn, the target can make an R test (9 + attacker's Power) to cancel the effect.

### Healing (1pt)
You can heal yourself, or someone you can touch. You spend 2MP to heal 1D HP, up to a dice limit equal to the Skill. For example, if you have H2 you can heal up to 2D HP in a character, spending 4MP. You can also spend 2MP for a character to repeat a Resistance test against some lasting negative effect (paralysis, poison, disease, annoyance...) after having failed.

### Special Defense (1pt each)
When receiving an attack, you can spend energy to activate some defense technique, such as a shield, force field, martial arts block or frown. Each effect has its MP cost: 

- Armored. Defense scores a critical hit on a 5 or 6. 1MP.
- Block. You defend using Power instead of Resistance. Any bonus or penalty that would affect your Resistance now affects Power. 1MP.
- Dodge. You defend using Skill instead of Resistance. Any bonus or penalty that would affect your Resistance now affects Skill. 1MP.
- Protection. You help an ally defend themselves. If a character near you is attacked, you can activate the advantage and use your defense instead of theirs, with any maneuvers or resources you want. If the attack still causes damage, it is the ally who receives it. 1MP per ally.
- Taunt. You draw an attack against an ally, receiving it in your place. If a character near you is attacked, you can activate the advantage and receive that attack in their place. You use your defense normally, with any maneuvers or resources you want. If the attack still causes damage, you are the one who receives it. 1MP.
- Reflection. You return the blow to the opponent. If your defense is higher than the opponent's attack, he suffers damage equal to the difference. 1MP.
- Robust. When you get a critical hit in defense, you can spend 2MP to add your Resistance once more.
- Tenant. Increases Resistance by +2 in defense. You can buy this effect more times (for example, 4pt for a Special Defense that spends 4MP and defends with +8). 1MP for each +2.
- Titanic. You surpass your limits and your potential! You have an automatic critical on your defense, and it will be considered perfect with any result. 3MP.

You can buy this advantage multiple times to have multiple effects. You can choose, at the time of defense, which effects you want to use or not. An Armored and Tenacious Defense ×2, for example, costs 3MP to use. For effects that reduce MP cost, a Special Defense with multiple effects is considered a single advantage.

### Wear and tear (1pt)
Even after the attack, your target takes more damage. Whether it's from poison, acid, burn, radiation, fatigue, stomachache, resentment or whatever you come up with.

Make an attack and spend 2MP. If you beat the target's defense and cause damage, he will take the same damage again in the next round.

The extra damage can be avoided if the target spends their next action to do so — by putting out the flames on their clothing, chanting a mantra, taking an antacid, or something similar.

### Devotee (1pt)
You serve a cause or belief. The object of your devotion is your choice. A higher spiritual entity, an ancient philosophy, the teachings of your former master, a solemn promise, a journey of revenge. Whatever it is, this great goal guides your actions and your life. When making a test while following or defending your devotion, you can spend 2MP to gain. For example, when a devotee of justice defends an injustice, or when a devotee of ecology protects an environmental preservation area. “Uh, I want to be a Devotee who always wins!” Do you REALLY think we haven’t thought of that? You can resort to devotion up to two times per scene. No more than two.

### Mental Link (1pt)
You have a special bond with another character, who can be a player or an NPC. He or she must also have this advantage.
You share the following powers:
- While you can see each other, you can perceive each other’s thoughts and communicate without any apparent signal.
- In dangerous situations, you can spend MP from your ally for your own use, and vice versa.
- When you have a Gain on a certain test, before rolling, you can choose to pass this Gain to the ally. He or she must use the Gain on the same type of test, at the first opportunity.

When you cannot see each other, you feel only general emotions, but you always know how far and in which direction the other is.

### Extend (1pt)
You can extend the effect of your advantages to your companions. When using a personal advantage (which affects only you), you can spend 1 MP per turn so that it also works on a Nearby ally. For example, you can use Teleport and take two other characters with you by spending an additional 2MP. Advantages that attack or harm other targets cannot be affected by Extend.

### Famous (1pt)
You are known around the world. When you make a test in social situations involving NPCs who recognize you (usually 1-4 in 1D), you can spend 3MP to receive Gain.

### Focus (1pt)
You can concentrate to improve your performance — whether it’s building up ki energy in your hands, honing your aim, gathering willpower, or thinking better to solve a puzzle.

Spend 2MP and spend a full turn concentrating. You have Defense Loss during this time, and if you take damage, you lose your concentration and the MP spent. On the next turn, your next test receives an automatic critical (stat added again). You can still roll more criticals normally.

Example: a character with P2 spends a turn preparing an infallible argument and, on the next turn, makes his Influence test. For this test, he will add his Power at least twice (2+2).

### Strong (1pt)

You are very strong. You receive P+2 on tests to undertake physical effort, such as lifting weights, breaking down a door, or punching a villain. This bonus is included in critical hits.
You can also spend 2MP before making one of these tests to get a critical hit on a 5 or 6.

### Genius (1pt)
You have a remarkable brain. You receive H+2 on tests to solve problems that involve knowledge, intelligence and reasoning ability. This bonus is included in critical hits.
You can also spend 2MP before making one of these tests to get a critical hit on a 5 or 6.

### Finishing Blow (1pt)
You have a powerful blow that can finish off an already weakened target.
Spend 3MP and make an attack against a target that is close to defeat. This attack is elevated one scale higher.

### Grimoire (1pt or more)
You have a spell grimoire, monastic scroll, recipe book, instruction manual, cipher magazine, textbook, mobile app, or other source of knowledge containing secrets of unique abilities. This advantage allows you to start with one or more techniques (see Rewards). Each Grimoire point is worth 10XP to acquire common tricks or techniques (but not legendary). The chosen techniques must receive authorization from the master.

### Illusion (2pt)
You can create illusions. An illusion is a three-dimensional image. It has no mass or substance, cannot be touched, nor can it suffer or cause damage (any touch or attack only passes through it). But it deceives the other senses as if it were real. In normal situations, one cannot perceive that something is an illusion. If one tries to interact with it (perhaps by touching it), or if it is something very strange and out of place, a character can make a Perception test. The standard goal for this test is 9 + the amount of MP spent on the illusion, but the master can change it if the illusion is very convincing (or very absurd). The Sense advantage also helps to avoid being deceived by an illusion. The cost to create an illusion depends on its size.

- 1MP. Something the size of a book or smaller.
- 3MP. The size of a person or smaller.
- 5MP. The size of a truck.
- 8MP. The size of a building.

Set costs with the GM for even larger or intermediate-sized illusions. An illusion can be created at any point you can see. Creating an illusion takes one action. Once created, illusions are immobile. Moving an illusion for a full round requires the character to spend one movement on his turn (the illusion only moves after you spend your movement).

You can have more than one illusion active at a time (but moving each one uses one movement). An illusion lasts as long as you want, but you cannot regain the MP used to create it until you dispel it.

The illusion also disappears if you are defeated.

### Imitate (1pt)
You can imitate one of your target's advantages. When someone uses an advantage in your presence (Far or less), you can try to imitate that advantage. Make a Perception check (9): if you succeed, you can spend 3MP to acquire the same advantage until the end of the scene. You can also imitate an advantage if you know in advance that the target has it. You may have already seen him use it, or discovered it because he is Telepathic, or already know about it because he is Famous or Infamous, or with a skill check (Knowledge, Streetwise, Perception, 12). In this case, in addition to spending MP, you must use an action. Only one advantage can be imitated at a time. If you successfully imitate another advantage, the first one will be released.

### Immortal (1pt)
You can be defeated, and even taken out of the scene for a while, but you can’t be killed. You’re like the hero who was beaten, decapitated, torn into a thousand pieces and thrown into the sea — only to resurface staggering on some beach later. How? It’s a mystery!
On death tests, you never have a result worse than unconscious.

### Improvisation (2pt)
In times of trouble, you can use abilities you shouldn’t have!
You remember some forgotten lesson, download apps from the cloud, receive enlightenment from the gods, or are such a good impostor that you fool even yourself.
You can spend 3MP to learn, on the spot, a skill you don’t have. You can use this skill until the end of the scene.
You can improvise only one skill at a time. If you improvise a new one, you’ll forget the previous one.

### Immune (1pt each)
You are immune to something that normally affects other creatures.
- Abiotic. You have no basic biological needs, such as eating, drinking, breathing, or sleeping (but you still need to rest to recover resources).
- Amphibious. You breathe and move normally underwater, unaffected by difficult terrain or underwater pressure. Disease. You are immune to natural or magical diseases.
- Resilient. You do not tire from exerting yourself strenuously for too long. In addition, any resting place is considered ideal.
- Mindless. You are immune to spells and effects that affect the mind, such as telepathy and other mental effects that only work on living creatures.

### Incorporeal (2pt)
You can become intangible. Immaterial, like a ghost. By spending an action and 5MP, you can pass through doors and other physical barriers, and you are also immune to any damage from violent combat — except Magic or attacks from other Incorporeal beings. Likewise, you also cannot interact physically with material objects nor
cause damage, except with Magic.

This effect lasts until the end of the scene, or until you decide to end it, or are defeated. Even intangible, you can suffer mental/emotional damage; words still hurt!

### Enemy (1-2pt each)
You are trained in facing a certain type of creature, knowing their powers and weaknesses.

Choose a group from the following:
- Humans.
- Humanoids. Amphibians, dwarves, centaurs, elves, goblins, hynne, kemono…
- Constructs. Androids, cyborgs, golems, mechas…
- Spirits. Abyssals, celestials, dahllan, fairies, ghosts, osteon, qareen, vampires…
- Monsters. Aberrants, aliens, kallyanach, kaiju…

In all tests against creatures of the chosen type, you get a critical hit on a 5 or 6.

The GM has the final say on who belongs to each group.

Some archetypes, such as medusas, minotaurs, ogres and sauroids, can be humanoids and also monsters.

The normal cost of the advantage is 1 point for each group, but it increases to 2pt if the group or creature exists in great abundance in the campaign (for example, aliens in Galaxy Wars, or humans almost anywhere).

Instead of a group, you can also choose a single archetype (except human), or even a single character. Against him, all your tests also gain a gain.

### Harmless (1pt)
You don’t look dangerous, for some reason. Maybe you look small, weak, or cowardly. Maybe you’re a cute little animal, a pot-bellied grandpa, a little girl with pigtails… you name it. In combat, by surprising your opponent, you gain an extra action before the first turn. Your harmless appearance doesn’t work on someone who’s seen you fight before, nor does it work twice on the same person. Even against them, however, you gain a gain when rolling initiative.

You can also spend 3MP to Gain in tests to try to deceive someone or go unnoticed.

### Instructor 1pt
You know how to teach or inspire another person to be successful in something. You pass on knowledge telepathically, sing a sublime song, threaten to make someone eat your slop, promise something irresistible, whisper some tips or something like that.

Using an action and spending 2MP, you allow an ally to make a test as if he had a skill that you have. If the ally already has the skill, you can allow him to use an advantage that you have that affects him, such as Mastery or Mentor. Other costs for using the advantage (such as MP) must be paid by the ally.

The test must be made by the ally's next turn; it is not possible to "save" uses for later.

### Inventory (1-3pt)
You carry consumable items with you to use in times of need. How many items the inventory contains depends on the cost:
- 0pt No inventory. Even without this advantage you can still carry two common items.
- 1pt Small. 3 common items and 1 uncommon.
- 2pt Large. 5 common items and 2 uncommon.
- 3pt Supreme. 5 common items, 4 uncommons and 1 rare.
You can trade three items of a lower tier for a higher tier, and vice versa. For example, three common items for one uncommon. It is not necessary to decide which items the Inventory carries; you choose at the time of use, reducing the total available. The Inventory is refilled at the beginning of each adventure, and other times throughout the session, at the GM’s discretion. More details on Inventory and consumable items in “Rewards”.

### Invisible (1-2pt)
You can use an action and spend 3MP to become invisible. When you are invisible, you have Gain on tests to hide, and all attacks against you have Loss. 1pt Your invisibility is interrupted when you make an attack (or any hostile action), whether successful or not, and when you take any damage. 2pt Your invisibility is interrupted only when you take damage. You can also undo your invisibility whenever you want, without spending an action or movement. An enemy with Sense can make a contested Perception check against your Hide check to try to find you even if you are invisible and negate the Loss for one round.

### Irresistible (1pt)
Your powers are harder to resist. When a target makes a check to avoid being affected by one of your advantages (such as Nullification, Confusion, Illusion, Paralysis, or Smite, for example), you can spend 2MP or more. For each 2MP spent, the goal of the check to resist increases by one step (+3). You must announce the use of this advantage (and how much you will spend) before the target makes his or her check.

### Mastery (1pt each)
Choose a skill you have. On tests of this skill, you can spend 1MP to have a critical hit with a 5 or 6. You can buy this advantage more times for other skills.

### Magic 2pt
You perform magic. You can manipulate mystical energies and control the elements to create supernatural effects. Magic!
By spending 1MP, you add +1 to any test. With 3MP, for example, you add +3 to your attack by shooting a fireball. Or conjure
an air shield that increases your defense.

You can also spend MP and use an action to grant this bonus to an ally you can reach. For example, imbuing an ally's sword with a magical aura that makes it sharper.

Note that, like other advantages, Magic can have physical or mental/social effects. A spell could be a giant, crushing sledgehammer, or a charm that influences people. It could also improve non-combat rolls, say, by spending 3MP to create a climbing rope to aid in an Athletics roll (which now gets +3). The maximum bonus you can generate is equal to your Skill. When attacking or defending with Magic, you can use the Mystic skill instead of Fighting (but you can still use Fighting if you prefer). You can also make Mystic rolls without spending MP for minor magical effects, such as lighting a room, cleaning a table, or heating food (but nothing that gives a bonus). 

### +Mana (1pt each)
You have more Mana Points, in addition to those granted by your Skill. Each time you buy this advantage you get +10MP. So if you have H4 (20MP) and pay 1 point for +Mana, you now have 30MP. You can buy this advantage multiple times, for further increases of +10MP.

### +Limbs (2pt)
You have one or more extra limbs, such as a second pair of arms, a prehensile tail, tentacles, snake hair, a flying jaw, a drone with a claw... In short, any appendage that can do things.
You can spend 3MP to perform a second action on your turn. This action can be combat or non-combat: the extra limb can leaf through a book for reference, hold a magnifying glass to track, juggle to distract - that is, help in ways that do not necessarily involve grabbing or punching. In some cases, the extra action can grant a Gain to your other action, as if you were providing assistance to yourself.

You can only buy this advantage once for any number of members: the effect is the same. You cannot perform more than one extra action per round.

### Mentor (1pt each)
Choose a skill that you have. You learned this skill from a master, who taught you several tricks and techniques. Even if you no longer have contact with him, the lessons remain present in your memories. Once per scene, you can remember a teaching and have Gain on a test of the chosen skill. In addition, techniques that have the Mentor's skill as a prerequisite cost -1MP (minimum 1MP). You can buy this advantage more times for other skills.

### Stubborn (1pt)
You are able to consume your own body, mind and soul to persevere over adversity and achieve the impossible. You can spend 1 point of P, H or R (your choice) as if it were 1 Action Point. When an attribute is reduced, the maximum limit of the corresponding resource also decreases accordingly. Attribute points can only be recovered by resting: 8 hours of sleep restores 1 point in each attribute.

### Paralysis (1pt)
You have a way to prevent the target from acting. Freezing, trapping with a net, hitting its pressure points, asking an unanswerable question, saying something so embarrassing that it freezes... you choose. Make an attack and spend 2MP. If you beat the target's defense, instead of taking damage, it is immobilized. A paralyzed target is helpless. Paralysis lasts until the end of the scene, or until the target takes damage. Each turn, the target can make an R test (6 + attacker's Power) to cancel the effect.

### Patron (1pt)
You serve a person, organization, or institution with more resources than you.
When you make a purchase test for something that helps you in your Patron’s mission, you can spend 1MP to receive a Gain. 

You also receive an extra consumable item of each rarity already available in your Inventory.

### Punishment (1-2pt)
You can impose a negative effect on a target. Cause it to receive a Curse, or make it Monstrous, or Slow, or something else fun (for you). When purchasing this advantage, also choose a disadvantage of the same value.

Make an attack and spend 2MP. If you beat the target’s defense, instead of taking damage, it suffers the effects of the chosen disadvantage.

The Punishment lasts until the end of the scene, or until the target takes damage. Each turn, the target can make an R test (6 + attacker's Power) to cancel the effect. You can buy Punishment more times for other disadvantages, but you can only impose one at a time on each opponent.

### Regeneration (1-2pt)
You are very, very annoying to defeat.
- 1pt At the start of your turn, you recover 1HP.
- 2pt At the start of your turn, you recover 3HP. 
In addition, when you make a death test, you never have a result worse than Unconscious. In this case, you awaken at the end of the scene and regenerate again. Oh yes, be careful with opponents who have Nullification. Hehe! 

### Resolute (1pt)
Your determination is unshakable. You receive R+2 on tests involving willpower, such as spotting deception, resisting charms and illusions (this includes death tests). This bonus is included on critical hits. You can also spend 2MP before making one of these tests to get a critical hit on a 5 or 6. Wealth 2, 4, or 6pt Bourgeois bastard! You have financial resources of a superior scale. When you make purchase tests, you can spend 2MP to increase your scale by one level. You can spend more points to be even richer, and you can spend more MP to receive a greater bonus.
- 2pt Sugoi. Your purchase test increases by one level (Gain and an additional critical). 2PM
- 4pt Kiodai. Increases by up to two levels (two Gains and two additional criticals). 4PM
- 6pt Kami — the wealth of a god! Increases by up to three levels (three Gains and three additional criticals). 6PM At higher levels, you can even choose to spend less MP for fewer scales. MP spent on this advantage cannot be regained until the end of the scene.

### Sense (1pt each)
You have a better or different sense. You can make Perception checks in situations that are impossible for others, such as seeing in the dark or noticing invisible things.

- Keen. Choose between hearing, smell, taste, touch, or sight. On Perception checks using this sense, you always gain.
- Infravision. You can perceive warm-blooded beings and also see in the dark, except near intense heat (flames, large engines, gunfire, etc.).
- Intuition. You can perceive other people’s hidden intentions, such as when they are lying, hiding their emotions, keeping secrets, intending to attack or betray you.
- Radar. You can perceive everything around you, even from behind or in the dark. You can also “hear” radio, TV, cell phone, Wi-Fi, and others.
- X-ray. You can see through doors and walls, except when made of very dense materials (such as metal) or magical.

Detecting something requires a Perception test: the better the result, the more you know about the direction, proximity, and location of what you want to find. Whenever a rule talks about something you can “see” (like Telepath and Teleport), it also goes for something you can perceive with a Sense. You can invent a unique special sense, such as being able to detect a specific type of material, creature, energy, or object. For example, you could have Ki Sense, Animal Sense, Danger Sense, Gold Sense, or something like that. However, you can’t invent a sense that is better than any other on the list. Each time you buy this advantage, you can choose something different (except for things forbidden by the GM). 

### Telepath (1pt)
You can read or sense other people’s thoughts. You can use a move and spend 1MP to: 
- Gain a social skill check related to a character in the scene.
- Make a Skill (9) check to learn something about a character in the scene (such as an ability score, an advantage, or a disadvantage). 
- Predict an opponent’s movements in combat; Gain defense against them until the next round. 

You can only use telepathy on living creatures that you can see. 

### Teleport (1pt)
You can use a move and spend MP to move to any location you can see. Each move you replace with Teleport costs 1MP. For example, going from Near to Very Far requires two moves; teleporting that distance costs 2MP. Teleportation is not affected by difficult terrain; you just need to be able to see the point you want to reach. If the destination location is observed by indirect means (for example, on television), the GM may rule that it is impossible, or require a check, or much higher MP expenditure. You can also spend 3MP to gain defense against an attack. 

### Cheerleader (1pt)
You are used to acting in the presence of many people cheering and motivating you. When a cheerleader is present, you have one Gain per round, on any test you want.
At the beginning of each scene, roll 1D. On a result of 1, there is some kind of cheerleader in favor of you — from ordinary passersby to some curious animal. The GM can judge special situations and waive the roll: there will be no cheerleaders for you in a desolate dungeon or enemy territory, but there almost always will be in a stadium or tournament.
In the absence of a cheerleader, an ally can replace it by using an action to motivate you. You receive the benefit on your next turn.

### Transformation (1-2pt each)
You can change form and powers. It wouldn't be an anime/tokusatsu game without this, right? Make another character, with the same number of points. It can be different in every way—it can even have a different personality, memories, and skills—except that it must also have this advantage, at the same value.

- 1pt If the new form has different disadvantages, all forms suffer the effects of all disadvantages combined.
- 2pt Each form suffers only the effects of its own disadvantages.

Changing form requires an action. The transformation restores your MP and HP, adjusted according to the attributes of the new form. You can transform only once per session, or pay 1AP for each additional transformation beyond the first.

### +Life (1pt each)
You have more Life Points, in addition to those provided by your Stamina. Each time you purchase this advantage, you receive +10HP. So, if you have R2 (10HP) and pay 1 point for +Life, you now have 20HP. You can purchase this advantage multiple times, for further increases of +10HP.

### Vigorous (1pt)
You are as robust as a horse. You receive R+2 on tests involving physical health, such as resisting fatigue, disease, and poison (this includes death tests). This bonus is included on critical hits.
You can also spend 2MP before making one of these tests to get a critical hit on a 5 or 6.

### Flight (1pt)
You can fly! When using a move to get Far or farther through the air, only characters who can also fly can use moves to reach you. Additionally, you ignore certain restrictions caused by difficult terrain.

In combat, taking flight while on the ground uses movement and costs 2MP. You do not need to spend more MP to stay in the air, but you do spend it if you want to take flight again after landing (or falling) for any reason. Flying for too long is subject to fatigue (see +Rules).

## Disadvantages (page 65)
While advantages are special qualities and powers that a character has, disadvantages hinder their life. They are personal obstacles to overcome. “But why, damn it, would I want something like that?” you ask. First, because no one is perfect — having problems makes your character more interesting, more like a movie, game or comic book hero.

Disadvantages are not defects, they do not make you “worse”; they make you different, persistent and intriguing.
Second, because taking disadvantages makes you earn points to spend on other things, such as attributes and advantages. This balance is the best way to achieve a balanced character.

A newly created character can accumulate up to –2 points in disadvantages. If you want, you can take disadvantages beyond this limit (perhaps for the purpose of fulfilling requirements, or just because you find it interesting), but without receiving more points.

### Environment (–1pt)
You are more accustomed to a certain environment: water, very hot or cold weather, methane atmosphere, shopping mall, home theater, arcade, fully equipped restaurant, or another place/climate/terrain that does not exist in abundance in the campaign location. At the beginning of each scene, roll 1D. On a result of 1, nothing related to your environment exists there, and you have Loss on all tests. The GM can judge special situations and waive the roll. For example, when your Environment is water and you are in a submerged city (in this case, it would be impossible for there to be no water in the scene). Likewise, if the group travels through the desert, there would be no water — and no need to roll the die. 

### Amnesia (–2pt)
You do not know who you are. Maybe you are a spy who was brainwashed, or an android with erased data, or some great trauma took away your memory. If you decide to have this disadvantage, the GM makes your sheet in secret. You can try to do things, and the GM says whether or not you succeeded — without ever saying whether you actually have the skill or advantage. You may gain or lose on tests without knowing the reason. Also, the GM may choose to roll your tests secretly, only announcing the results. Don’t ask! You may eventually figure things out through trial and error, but your full capabilities and limitations should remain a mystery.

“Oh, I’ll try everything!” Nice try, smart guy, but your advantages only work in situations of real risk, and the disadvantages appear at the worst possible moment.

Warning: This disadvantage is fun, but it’s a lot of work for the GM. Be nice to him. Bring soda.

### Unfriendly (–1pt)
You can’t (or don’t want to) express yourself well. It could be due to shyness, inelegantness, pride, grumpiness, an aura of sinister energy, or you’re just a real pain in the ass! When you make a Power test involving social interaction, you have a Loss and never have critical hits. You can’t be Unfriendly and also Charismatic.

### Haunted (–1 or –2pt)
You are haunted by a spirit, bad feeling, bitter memory or something else that takes away your concentration. –1pt When you enter a situation where the haunting could affect you—such as a combat or action scene—roll a die. On a result of 1, all your tests are Loss until the end of the scene. –2pt The haunting appears on any odd result.

### Clumsy (–1pt)
You are easily fumbled. When you make a Skill test involving coordination and agility, you have Loss and you never have critical hits. You cannot be both Clumsy and Agile.

### Aura (–1 or -2pt)
You emanate a heavy aura of negative energy, electromagnetic static, bad karma, foul odor, or pure smut. Anyone can sense it and realize that you are the source, causing Loss on social tests. –1pt Any test by others made Near you, whether allies or enemies, has Loss. –2pt Any test Far from you or closer. “Oh, it doesn’t affect me, it’s not my problem, I’ll get it.” Try it, dude! Good luck trying to play this team game.

### Battery (–1pt)
You have a reserve of energy to stay active. Whenever you reach 0MP you “shut down”, becoming unconscious and defenseless.
Also, when you are low on MP (below your H), your battery is low and all your tests have Loss.

### Code  (–1pt each)
You follow a code of conduct that prevents you from doing (or not doing) something. Whenever you violate your code, guilt leaves you with a Loss on all tests, until you can redeem yourself in some way (by making up for the harm
you caused, or by fulfilling the code at the next opportunity).

- Asimov’s 1st Law. Never harm a human being or, through inaction, allow a human to come to harm.
- Asimov’s 2nd Law. Always obey human beings, except when their orders violate another Code you have.
- Code of the Hunter. Never kill (only defeat) cubs or pregnant animals. Never abandon a kill you’ve killed. Always choose the most dangerous enemy on the scene as your opponent. Never allow an opponent to escape.
- Code of Combat. Never attack an opponent in an unfavorable condition (at a Loss) or of inferior rank.
- Code of Defeat. Never continue fighting after defeat. Accept any condition of the victor, which may be to owe a favor, become his servant/ally, or take his life.
- Code of Gratitude. Whenever someone does a great favor (such as saving your life, freeing you from captivity…) to you or a loved one, you remain at the service of that person until you can return the favor (save your life, free him in the same way…).
- Code of Heroes. Always keep your word, always protect any person or creature weaker than you, never refuse a request for help.
- Code of Honesty. Never lie, steal, cheat or disobey local laws, nor allow your companions to do so.
- Code of Redemption. Never attack first, always accept a surrender request, always spare defeated opponents.

You can have more than one Code.

### Dependency (–2pt)
The character depends on something rare, forbidden, or inhumane to live. It could be a vampire who drinks blood, a zombie who devours brains, or an addict to some illicit substance — it will almost always be something that involves a serious crime.
The Dependency must be satisfied every day. If you don’t, the character suffers Loss on all tests until he satisfies the Dependency.
This disadvantage is for NPC villains. It’s not for you. Don’t get involved.

### Different (–1pt)
Your body is very different from a normal humanoid body. Much larger, smaller, with extra limbs, or in unusual places, or for some other reason. You have trouble using the weapons, clothing, equipment, machines, and vehicles available to you — you can only use those made specifically for you.
The reverse is also true: things made for you don’t work well for other characters. Possessions chosen during character creation (Relic, Utensil, etc.) are made for you.
If you receive this disadvantage because you are a member of a fantastic species (goblin, hynne, ogre, etc.), then you can use items made for members of the same race. You have Loss when using items made for others. On the other hand, the GM may decide that your body is advantageous in some situations — a small goblin would have an easier time sneaking around with a Streetwise check. In these special cases, you may even gain Gain. 

### Life Link (–1pt)
Choose an ally, who must also have this disadvantage. Whenever one of you takes damage, the other also loses HP by the same amount. If the characters belong to different scales, the damage is adjusted proportionally. Like when a giant robot takes damage and its pilot is also injured. The opposite is not true: healing one does not heal the other. 

### Weakling (–1pt)
You are weak, lacking in hatred… and strength. When making a Power check involving physical effort, such as moving a boulder or breaking down a door, you have Loss and never have critical hits. You cannot be Weakling and also Strong. 

### Frail (–1pt)
You have little physical stamina. When making a Resistance test involving physical health, such as resisting disease, poison, or death tests, you have Loss and never have critical hits.
You cannot be Frail and also Vigorous.

### Weakness (–1 or -2pt)
There is an object, element, or condition to which you are especially vulnerable. While you are Near that thing, all your tests have Loss. The amount of disadvantage depends on how common the source of the Weakness is:

- Incomum (-1pt). A Fraqueza é algo que aparece raramente (1 em 1D). Livros. Espelhos. Gatos. Objetos de bronze. Cor roxa. Gente de chapéu. Aquela pedra verde brilhante.
- Comum (-2pt). A Fraqueza é algo que aparece mais frequentemente (1-3 em 1D). Lugares escuros (ou iluminados). Vento. Água. Plantas. Cor vermelha.

### Rage (–2pt)
Whenever you take damage or become angry for any reason (at the GM's discretion), you must make a Resistance test (9 or equal to the damage, whichever is greater). If you fail, you go into battle frenzy and immediately attack the target of your anger. If you fail, you attack the nearest poor thing.

During the Rage, all your non-attack tests are Loss, including defense tests. In addition, all your advantages cost double MP.

The Rage ends when you or your opponent is defeated, or if the opponent manages to escape. You are then immediately exhausted (your MP drops to zero and you have Loss on all tests until you rest).

### Inept (–1pt)
Choose a skill you do not have. You are completely incompetent when it comes to it. Whenever the GM asks for a test with the chosen skill, you are at a Loss. Furthermore, any failure is considered a critical failure (as if all results were 1). Now, if on top of all that you actually roll a critical failure… Ok, the less said about that, the better.

### Uneducated (–1pt)
You are unfamiliar with the local culture. You may be a foreigner (alien?), have just arrived from a Convergence, have not received proper education, are very naive, or were literally born yesterday.
You cannot read the local language, or find it very difficult to do so (Hard test).
You have the same difficulty communicating, except with your allies — and even with them the GM may sometimes ask for tests to understand you. Social tests with characters who do not understand you are Loss.

### Indecisive (–1pt)
You have little conviction and great difficulty making important decisions. On Willpower Saving Throws, you are Loss and never have critical hits.
You cannot be both Indecisive and Resolute.

### Infamous (–1pt)
You are well-known, but not for good reason. When you make social checks related to an NPC that recognizes you (usually 1-4 in 1D), you are always at a Loss. You cannot be both Infamous and Famous.

### Slow (–1pt)
You move slowly. On initiative checks, you are always at a Loss, and you also spend one extra movement to cross each distance. You cannot be both Slow and Haste.

### Curse (–1 or -2pt)
You are the victim of a curse that disturbs you almost all the time. The exact nature and effect can be invented by you, but must be authorized by the GM.

- –1pt The Curse is mild, more annoying or embarrassing than anything else. For example, a pesky magical creature chases you. Your advantages only work when you take off your clothes. You change sex when splashed with cold/hot water. It usually has no effect on the rules, but in some cases it can distract you and cause a Loss.
- –2pt The Curse is very serious, challenging. For example, whenever you cause damage to someone, you receive the same damage back. In tests of an attribute chosen by the master, all your failures are critical failures. You can never heal HP except by resting. A monster attacks you every night while you sleep.

### Monstrous (–1pt)
You have a grotesque appearance, which causes repulsion to those who can see you. Other characters are always ready to fight you: except when you take them by surprise, you always have Loss in initiative tests.

You also have Loss on social tests that involve appearance.

You can’t be both Monstrous and Harmless.

### Ammunition (–1pt)
Your weapon or technique requires some kind of reload each time it’s used. This could be bullets, arrows, and other projectiles, or your own ki energy, or mental power for another chess move, or catching your breath for another blast of fire or torrent of profanity.

Whenever you use an action to attack, you must use a move to reload. If you make an attack without reloading, you don’t add your Power to the attack roll, nor can you add it back on critical hits.

### Pacifist (–1 or -2pt)
You reject conflict and violence.
- –1pt You do not engage in violent combat. When attacked, you can defend yourself, but do not make attack rolls. You can still reduce HP and defeat your opponents in other ways (see Fighting Isn’t Everything).
- –2pt You do absolutely nothing that causes harm — even wordly harm is too violent and offensive for you!

If you violate your pacifism, the guilt will leave you in Loss until you can redeem yourself in some way.

### Poverty (–1pt)
You have no financial resources and, for whatever reason, tend not to be able to accumulate them in any way. All your purchase rolls are made in Loss. You cannot have Poverty and also Wealth.

### Weakness (–1pt)
You have a trait that your opponent can exploit. It could be an imbalance in your fighting stance, a nervous tic, a flaw in your train of thought, or you lose your temper whenever someone mentions that past incident… Your Weak Point can be discovered by someone who has seen you in action, or by Telepathy, or be known to everyone if you are Famous or Infamous. An opposed Perception test against the skill you are using also allows you to discover it. Any opponent who knows your Weak Point can spend 1MP to gain against you in a test that exploits this problem.

### Protected (–1pt)
There is someone you need to protect at all costs, with your own life if necessary. It could be another player character, or an NPC who accompanies you. Keeping a Protected is risky, villains can (will!) try to use it as blackmail to defeat you. Whenever the Warded is missing, defenseless, or injured (any amount of HP below the total), all of your tests are Loss.
If the Warded dies or disappears forever, you acquire the severe version of Haunted (–2pt). “Oh, but do I get the difference point?” NO!

### Restriction (–1 or -2pt)
A certain condition makes it harder to use your powers. When it occurs, you always spend twice as much MP to use your advantages.
The value of the disadvantage depends on how common the Restriction is:
- Uncommon (–1pt). The Restriction happens rarely. You are wet. You are hungry. It is a full moon night. The enemy belongs to a certain archetype (human, elf, dwarf…). Someone in the group farted.
- Common (–2pt). The Restriction happens very often. It is night (or day). You are in an enclosed (or open) place. Someone is watching. The annoying bard kept singing. Someone in the group farted (each one knows their group). You cannot have this disadvantage if you do not have any advantages that cost MP. Shame on you!

### No Life (–2pt)
You are not a living being. You could be a construct created by advanced science, an undead creature animated by negative energy, a beloved toy that gained a soul, or an ancient relic that awakened a spirit. Or a talking shoe. Yeah. The good news is that you cannot die; on death rolls, you never have a result worse than unconscious. The bad news is that you do not recover HP in normal ways. Neither can you rest, nor can you use items, nor can you use advantages such as Healing and Regeneration. You can be repaired or restored with another skill, such as Machines (for constructs) or Mystic (for undead), performed by another character. Each attempt requires one hour of work (repairing or producing magical elixirs) and a roll (9). If successful, your HP returns to full. You still need to rest to recover AP and MP.

### Dumb (–1pt)
You are not a very bright person. When you make Skill rolls related to intelligence and reasoning, you have Loss and you never have critical hits. You cannot be Dumb and also a Genius.

### Disorder (–1pt each)
You deal with a mental disorder. Choose one from the following:
- Kleptomania. You steal things you don’t need, not because of their value, just because they’re interesting. Whenever you have the chance to steal something, you must make a check to avoid it. A kleptomaniac never returns the proceeds of his/her thefts to their owners, and will fight to prevent this from happening.
- Compulsion. There is something you must do several times a day: take a shower, do laundry, eat lasagna, play the harp, bite your nails, read comics… Each time a scene starts, you have Loss on all checks until you spend a round fulfilling your compulsion.
- Distraction. You can’t focus on something that doesn’t interest you (anything unrelated to Devotee or Code). In these situations, you can’t score critical hits.
- Fantasy. You believe you are someone or something you’re not, or you think you can do something you’re not capable of. (I’m a wizard! I can fly! I’m Captain Ninja!) You talk about yourself all the time to announce your fantasy to everyone around you. You fail all social tests.
- Phobia. You are terribly afraid of something (high places, insects, water, blood…). Whenever that thing is Near, you can’t perform actions, only movements. You can only perform an action if you first pass a test.
- Megalomania. You believe you are invincible, immortal, destined to achieve a great goal, and no one will be able to stop you! You never give up and keep fighting even after being defeated.
- Mythomania. You never tell the truth about anything, even when you want to. You need a test to tell the truth, even to your allies.
- Paranoia. You don’t trust anyone, not even your allies. You need a test to accept any help (even healing!). In addition, you don’t sleep well: your resource recovery with rest is half of normal.

When overcoming the disorder requires a test, it is a Resistance test (9).

### Tool (–1 or -2pt)
You need an item (weapon, amulet, tool, cell phone…) to properly use a skill or advantages.
- –1pt Choose a skill that you have. You need the Tool to properly use this skill. When you don’t have this item, you can’t get critical hits.
- –2pt Without the Tool, you can’t use advantages or techniques that spend MP (you can’t choose this disadvantage if you don’t have the means to spend MP). You also cannot use AP except for the purpose of retrieving the Tool or improvising it (see below).

If you roll a critical failure while using the Tool, you drop it and must spend a move to retrieve it.

If an attacker rolls a critical hit against you, instead of increasing the damage, he may choose to destroy your Tool. In this case, you lose the item, but take no damage from the attack.

If the Tool is destroyed or lost, you get another one just like it after the scene ends. Or, you can spend an action and 1AP to improvise it.

You can also choose something large as a Tool, such as a trailer, library, or workshop. In this case, you cannot drop it, lose it, or destroy it (hopefully!), but you also cannot carry it with you at all times.

### Archetypes (page 76)
Do you already have an amazing idea for your character? Do you know who he is, what he is? Have you chosen all the skills, advantages and disadvantages? Not yet?

Maybe you need a starting point, a base to start from. Archetypes are here for that.

**An archetype is something your character can be.** A people different from humans, a specific ethnicity within a broader species. An alien, centaur, elf, another fantastic, artificial or supernatural being. It's like a race or people in other RPGs, but not the same.

These archetypes represent the most common creatures in Age of Arcas. They are the beings that appear most frequently in the stories of this world, but not the only ones — in fact, anything can exist in 3DeT Victory. You don't have to adopt an archetype if you don't want to, but they make your task easier; part of the character's construction, including a bit of his background, is already done. Each archetype has a description and a small set of powers and weaknesses. Some correspond to existing advantages and disadvantages; others do not, and are unique.

**You cannot have more than one archetype.** No one can be a goblin-dwarf, an elf-construct, or a hynne-centaur. (That is, not usually! Ask your GM. If he lets you, it’s his business.)

You cannot buy powers from an archetype separately (except those that already exist as advantages or disadvantages). It’s all or nothing.

Disadvantages within an archetype do not count toward the –2 limit on disadvantages, but you also cannot trade them or pay points to get rid of them.

A character with Transformation can have one archetype for each form; If you only have the 1-point version, you accumulate the problems of all the archetypes.

#### Human (0pt)
Even though the Convergences have brought countless fantastic creatures over the years, the human population still outnumbers all other peoples combined in numbers. Compared to other peoples, humans have more initiative, more energy, and are more adaptable. On the other hand, humans have short lives (usually shortened by sheer lack of judgment).

Also note that the Convergences didn’t just bring elves, dwarves, and minotaurs — they brought humans too! These Travelers are almost always more daring, skillful, and rash than Terran humans. Apparently, the place they came from was a world of great adventures. Or great problems!

Being human in the Age of Arks does not mean being normal at all! Due to their enormous variety of body types and ways of thinking, humans can be practically anything. They can follow all existing careers and invent others that do not yet exist. They can become super-powerful with rigorous training, or not-so-rigorous training (but it makes your hair fall out), or even by accident! The greatest heroes and villains of this time are not aliens or monsters: they are humans. Generally speaking, in game terms, a character without any other archetype is considered human (except when the GM or the character creator says otherwise). Characters who are descendants of humans with other races can freely adopt the archetype of either parent.

**Beyond** Humans surpass themselves, do not accept or know limits (not even when they should). Once per scene, you can spend 2MP to Gain a test.

#### Aberrant (1pt)
Aberrants are beings that are not part of this universe, they do not belong to the natural world. Even on Earth after the Third Convergence, with its technology, magic and fantastic species, aberrants are too bizarre.
Aberrants come in many forms: insane scientific experiments, mutations due to exposure to toxic waste, magical or alien diseases, corruption by extraplanar cosmic entities beyond comprehension... Likewise, their appearance and anatomy can vary. Countless eyes all over their bodies, mouths dripping with mucus, multiple tentacles instead of arms, bloody carapace crusts, luminescent gelatinous green skin... The Ark Age is not kind to aberrants. These often reciprocate this feeling with revolt or resignation. But others persevere, embrace overcoming, and fight to prove their worth. They fight for a world that fears and hates them.

**Deformity** Every aberrant has some physical deformity that, although unpleasant, grants certain advantages. Choose a skill. When you test it (even if you don’t have the skill), the corresponding attribute has +1. 

**Teratism** Aberrants have anomalous powers. You receive a Common Technique (see Rewards, page 142, and translate from there because Gabriel is tired already) of your choice. You still need to fulfill the technique’s requirements. 

**Monstrous** Trust me, no one deserves this disadvantage more than you. Sorry!

#### Abyssal (1pt)
As Convergences occur throughout history, the barrier between the material plane and other dimensions weakens. Some of these dimensions are planes of an evil nature, so to speak. Places such as the Abyss, the Nine Hells, Hades, the Dark World, and others that are even less pleasant. Beings native to these planes are abyssals.

Also known as demons, devils, infernals, or oni, these extraplanar creatures visit Earth for various purposes, such as collecting souls (which they use as food, energy sources, or currency), hunting for treasures, chasing fugitives, macabre invocations, martial arts tournaments, impossible romances, or even “tourism.”

Many abyssals are servants to a powerful lord, evil monster-god, or other patron, but others are independent, with goals of their own. Several are adventurers and Arcanauts. And some aren’t even really evil.

**Agile** Demons are as elusive as cats. Who are also demons.

**Disfavor** You can spend an action and 3MP to force a creature within Close range to make a Saving Throw, opposed to your Power. If it fails, the target suffers Loss on all saves until its next turn.

**Infamous** You’re a demon, what did you expect?

#### Alien (1pt)
An alien does not have to be someone from another planet — it can be any “foreigner” from other times or dimensions.
Due to their peculiar origins, aliens can have very varied and unpredictable appearances and powers. Almost all of them are superior to humans in one or more aspects, but also inferior in others.

Whether due to parallel evolution or a whim of the universe, many aliens have a human appearance, except for some details (pointed ears, antennae, monkey tails, etc.) that are not very difficult to disguise. Even so, aliens have difficulty going unnoticed: the newcomer has trouble adapting to our culture and society, making mistakes and attracting attention (and trouble). Even an alien raised on Earth since childhood can feel out of place among the others, without knowing the truth about his origins and powers.

**Talent** Aliens have superior physical or mental abilities. Choose an advantage from Agile, Charismatic, Strong, Genius, Resolute or Vigorous.

**Xenobiology** Choose an advantage you have. You can use it for half the MP cost.

**Uncultured** Aliens are incapable of our culture. No, don't come claiming that "they are intelligent and superior pipipi popopo". Would you know how to behave in a Neanderthal tribe?!

#### Dwarf (1pt)
Dwarves do not exceed 1.30m in height, but are more robust than humans. They can use the same equipment and vehicles; what does not fit, they hammer until it does.

Dwarves tend to enjoy hard and meticulous work, some are among the greatest engineers and jewelers in the world. Their arrival on Earth, in fact, awakened in them a special passion for motorcycling; many are members of motorcycle clubs, and even more common are adventurous dwarves riding on long roads, piloting powerful machines in search of the next Ark.

Even in the most cosmopolitan cities, dwarves like to wear heavy metal accessories, even parts of armor. In addition to growing long and proud beards, dwarves also love beer, consuming many barrels throughout their long lives.

**Abascanto** Dwarves are stubbornly resistant to harmful effects. You have Gain on Resistance tests to avoid or cancel bad effects (except damage), such as the advantages Nullification, Wear and Tear, Paralysis and others.

**Iron and Fire** Dwarves love to forge metal. Whenever you test Machines (even if you don’t have the skill), the corresponding attribute has +1. In addition, you gain Sense (Infravision).

**Slow** You have short little legs. There’s no point in getting nervous, you won’t catch me!

#### Amphibian 1pt
There are numerous aquatic beings in the Age of Arks. Many belong to diverse peoples, such as mermen, mermaids, sea elves, frogmen...

Others may be mutants, or created in laboratories, to work in the depths or colonize oceanic worlds. Still others
came from underwater cities or even countries, many of which are still unknown to humans, while others have already signed trade treaties and even receive crowds of tourists.

Amphibians can live and act out of water normally; those with fish tails grow legs when they are on land. Some amphibians fear or despise inhabitants of the "dry world", while others see Earth as a mysterious and extravagant world, full of opportunities and adventures. When they find their place in today’s society, amphibians find themselves in obvious careers such as lifeguards, aquarium keepers, and marine biologists. But there’s no shortage of those who dedicate themselves to unexpected jobs or hobbies, such as extreme sportsmen, content producers, journalists, models, reality show contestants, and pro-gamers. When they become Arcanauts, amphibians are creative in exploiting their natural aquatic abilities while preventing their weaknesses from harming the group.

**Immune (Amphibian)** Underwater, you can move and breathe normally. You can also orient yourself in the dark, perceiving shapes (but not colors).

**Vigorous** Because of their tolerance to pressure changes, amphibians are more resilient.

**Environment** This disadvantage was practically invented for you.

#### Celestial (1pt)
Just as there are travelers who came from infernal dimensions, there are those known as celestials, angels or tenshi, natives of benign higher planes. Many have human appearance, but are very beautiful and
imposing, often emanating a luminous aura of positive energy. Others may have the appearance of animals, whether natural, such as lions and bears, or fantastic, such as pegasi and unicorns. Still others have mystical and incomprehensible forms, such as diagrams or geometric figures. Many are noble and kind, but not all. Celestials who visit Earth often make voluntary journeys, through spells, magical portals or natural abilities. Others are summoned (or captured) by powerful conjurers or supernatural phenomena.

**Charismatic** Celestials have an easy time influencing people.
**Captivating** You can spend a movement and 3MP to grant Gain to an ally’s skill check Near or Far. This check must be made before the ally’s next turn; it cannot “save” for later.
**Code** Every celestial is guided by at least one code of conduct.

#### Centaur (2pt)
This archetype represents any “tauric” creature, which has a human torso attached to a body with four or more legs. Typical centaurs are half human, half horse. But there are also rarer variations of half-lions, lizards, spiders, scorpions and others.

Legends of centaurs are ancient, suggesting that they have visited Earth since the first Convergences, almost always choosing to live as recluse in the wilderness. Some are friendly, helping lost people return home. But there are those who are territorial and dangerous, acting as predators. And many others embrace life in big cities, as far as their corpulence allows.

**Tauric Body** You can pay 1 MP to get a critical with 5 or 6 on Power tests for physical effort (lifting weight, breaking down a door, etc.; but not on attack tests), initiative tests, and Skill tests to run or chase a target.

**Vigorous** Centaurs are robust and resilient.

**Different** Obviously, centaurs cannot use clothing, vehicles, or equipment made for humans. The GM may make exceptions for weapons or items for the arms/head/torso.

#### Cyborg (2pt)
Comes from “cyborg”, short for “cybernetic organism”. A cyborg is part machine, part living being, bringing together the best of both worlds.

Cyborgs are humans (or other beings) with robotic parts, which can be just a few limbs or almost the entire body, leaving only the nervous system. There are also machines with organic parts, like those terminators of the future. Furthermore, this archetype also represents half-golems; creatures that have received artificial magical parts to replace certain natural parts.

Although cyborgs are almost always combat-oriented, they
can also end up successful in unexpected areas, from chefs to executives.

**Living Construct** You can recover HP normally, but you can also be repaired by another character, both methods work. See the No Life disadvantage.

**Immune** You are Immune to a lot of things: Abiotic, Diseases, Resilient.

**Directive** Your mind has programming locks. Choose a Code or Disorder.

#### Construct (1pt)
You were not born; you were constructed. In the Age of the Arks, there are several types of artificial beings.

Many are androids, mass-produced as workers, soldiers, or servants — but by being fed elemental crystals extracted from the Arks, some acquire minds and wills of their own. Most have an artificial, mechanical appearance, but there are also those that are almost identical to people, making it almost impossible to tell the difference (you may not even know it yourself). Still others are golems, magical creatures that came with the Convergences, made of exotic materials and animated by magic.

So, although there are robots of many sizes, shapes, and powers, this archetype represents only constructs of human shape and size (you can add other advantages or disadvantages to have a different form).

**Immune (Abiotic, Diseases, Resilient, Mindless)** Constructs have no biological functions, nor are they subject to certain limitations of living beings.

**Battery** Constructs do not eat, drink, or sleep, but they still need to recharge energy. This can be a lithium battery, a gas tank, a crystal charged with magical energy, or something else of your choice. 

**No Life** Constructs cannot be healed, only repaired.

#### Dahllan (1pt)
Part human, part fairy, and part plant, the Dahllan are a people with tree sap running through their veins. Some were born of human parents (travelers or not) and forest fairies, here or beyond the Convergence.

Others simply emerge from the deep woods, raised by animals, without even knowing their true origins. Almost all are female, although other genders are suspected.

Dahllan have varied body types. Some are muscular, others slender, still others are curvy or overweight. Their hair, like the leaves of the trees, can be green, red, gold—or change with the seasons. It is not uncommon to see flowers blooming in their hair, as well as the occasional butterfly or ladybug coming from who knows where. Although they prefer the wilderness or rural areas, dahllan can be seen in cities, often in the pharmaceutical industry, environmental engineering, park management or pet market.

**Nature's Blessing** You can spend one movement and 2MP to transform your skin into tree bark, gaining Gain on all Defense rolls until your next turn.

**Wild Empathy** Every dahllan has an intrinsic connection with natural or magical animals. In all Animal tests (even if they do not have the skill), the corresponding attribute has +1.

**Dahllan Code** Dahllan are vegan. In addition, they never attack an animal, nor do they fail to help an animal in difficulty.

#### Elf (1pt)

Elves are beautiful and slender; they have pointed ears, almond-shaped eyes, delicate features, a talent for magic, and very long lives. They can have the same skin colors as humans, but some are white, blue, or green. Eyes and hair vary even more, and some have unusual features, such as paws and/or tails.

Socially, there are those who live isolated in nature, alone or in elven communities; and those who are fully integrated into human cities, which you can find in any coffee shop, with a formal job and bills to pay.

Many deal with magic, others are fearsome warriors. But, no matter the path, elves are artists; almost every minstrel group has at least one elf, even if only to attract an audience!

**Impeccable** Elves are elegant. Choose an advantage between Agile, Charismatic, or Genius.

**Mystical Nature** Even without magical powers, every elf has an intrinsic connection with magic. In all Mystical tests
(even if they do not have the skill), the corresponding attribute has +1.

**Fragile** Elves have delicate physical build, being vulnerable to harmful effects.

#### Fairy (1pt)
Fairy is just a generic name for several magical beings linked to aspects of nature, such as forests, waters, mountains, winds, animals, seasons, and others. They come in many types and forms, with many names: dryads, sylphs, sylphs, elves, satyrs, nymphs...

In common, almost all fairies have a feminine, childlike or androgynous appearance. Many are tiny, no bigger than cats, but many others are human-sized. Many have flowers or leaves growing on their bodies, forming their clothing. Those capable of flying have wings like dragonflies, butterflies, or made of leaves. Many have black, shiny eyes, like insects. Others have insect antennae. Fairies tend to be curious and playful by nature, always looking for some fun, leading everyone to underestimate them when the subject requires seriousness. However, it is also true that they can reach centuries of age and hide great wisdom.

**Fairy Magic** Choose between Magic or Illusion. In addition to gaining the advantage, you always spend –1MP (minimum 1MP) to use it. You can buy the other advantage separately, but without MP discount.

**Infamous** Fairies are not taken very seriously.

**Delicate** Choose between Different (a tiny fairy) or Fragile (normal size)

#### Ghost (2pt)
In the Age of Arks, some people believe that ghosts exist. Everyone else is certain. Ghosts are immaterial undead, disembodied spirits. When they appear, it is usually because some powerful or particular event prevented a deceased soul from finding eternal rest.

Ghosts can pass themselves off as living people — except to the touch, of course. They also reveal their true nature when they use any power that consumes Mana Points; at that moment they become translucent, semi-transparent.

**Spirit** Ghosts have an inverted version of Incorporeal; they are always in an immaterial state, unable to cause or receive damage, but they can spend MP to become solid for a scene. In addition, a ghost is Immune (abiotic, diseases) and Lifeless (only recovers HP with rest or advantages).

**Paralysis** You can paralyze victims with your aura of panic.

**Devout** A ghost is trapped in this world by love, anger, revenge, a promise, or reasons he himself does not understand, always engaged in an endless quest.

#### Goblin (1pt)
Goblins are small humanoids (about 1m tall) with gray, wrinkled skin, long ears, and yellow or red eyes that glow in the dark. They are also the most numerous among traveling peoples, reproducing quickly. Except for humans, they are the ones who form the largest communities on the planet — there are neighborhoods, cities, and even corporations composed only of goblins. It is said that the emergence of a goblinoid country is only a matter of time.

When the goblins arrived, many feared them as evil creatures, or even plagues; this is still the case in some parts of the world. However, with ingenuity and persistence, they ended up finding their place in human cities. Goblins are curious, hyperactive, and always find a solution to any problem — even if the result is not very pretty. Some say that it takes a special sensitivity to appreciate the goblinoid aesthetic sense; for others, goblins simply have no aesthetic sense at all. Or any other sense. Goblinoid logic is different and challenging, as if they had access to physical laws that we do not know (or just choose to ignore the ones we do know). A goblin invention that should not work at all, does. A machine that should do nothing, performs a thousand functions. Or explodes. Goblins accept explosions as satisfactory results. Almost all goblins on Earth seem quite happy, even those in precarious and poorly paid jobs, or in danger as adventurers on the Arks, or achieving high positions in large corporations with their cleverness. There is a suspicion that, in the afterlife of Convergence, they were not very well treated. Or liked.

**Smart** Goblins know how to get by. Whenever you test Streetwise (even if you don’t have the skill), the corresponding attribute has +1.

**Subterranean** You gain Sense (Infravision). You also gain Gain on R tests to avoid or cancel diseases and poisons, whether natural or magical.

**Different** Goblins are small and, to be honest, they don’t care much about dressing well.

#### Hynne 1pt
Also known as halflings or “little ones”, these beings are no taller than 90cm, about half the height of a human being.
Many also have hairy feet, preferring to go barefoot.

Hynne can be as small in ambition as they are in size; they usually enjoy a quiet life, without great risks, adopting stable jobs and comfortable careers. Hospitable and fond of good food and drink, some become excellent chefs, or managers in restaurants, banks or hotels. Others seek bucolic lives in rural areas — which, some say, remind them of their homes in the afterlife Convergence.

And still others, with their small agile fingers, are successful in e-sports; nothing more attractive to a hynne than living adventures and competing in competitions without leaving their chubby butts in the gaming chair!

But not all are like that. Some, restless, flee from comfort and seek great adventures, journeys, and being with friends. They use their quick hands and harmless appearance to help their colleagues. Then they return with fortunes to spend on banquets and parties!

**Marksman** Hynne have a natural aptitude for throwing things. You can spend 2MP to gain a Gain on a test to attack from a distance, in violent combat.

**Charming** The hynne's innocent smile gets what it wants. Whenever you test Influence (even if you don't have the skill), the corresponding attribute has +1.

**Different** When a hynne spends the night in your house, it can sleep in an open drawer.

#### Kallyanach (2pt)
Not all half-dragons — or kallyanach — are the result of romance between dragons and other peoples. Some were born that way because their parents lived near a dragon’s lair, such was their elemental energy. Others were victims of curses. Still others, on Earth, are the result of experiments with captive dragons in secret laboratories. Or they are simply born into human families for unknown reasons.

The typical half-dragon has claws, fangs, a tail, and/or scales. Some have large leathery wings. Many are elegant and beautiful, others are monstrous. Because they are rare, they do not usually form families or societies. They seek goals for their lives, or clues about their origins, and end up as adventurers. Or villains.

**Breath** You gain a Special Attack (choose between Area, Ranged, or Powerful). This attack can be combined with others, and always costs –1MP (minimum 1MP).

**Dragon Power** Dragons are powerful in physical strength and force of personality. Choose one between Strong and Charismatic.

**Code of the Dragons** Whether out of honor, gratitude, revenge or pride, a half-dragon has the natural drive to never forget and always fulfill a promise made.

#### Kemono (1pt)
Several beings that came with the Convergences are anthropomorphic animals — that is, with human characteristics. They are animals that walk, talk and act like humans; or humanoids with heads and/or other feral traits, such as ears, tails, claws, fangs, horns or fur.

The typical kemono, except for their appearance, is absolutely human: they speak languages, wear clothes, cook dishes and live in houses. Many, in fact, are offended if treated like animals. Their behavior is as civilized and integrated into society as any human.

The best known among the kemono are called moreau; there is evidence that they had their own society beyond the Convergence, ruling kingdoms, even advancing their own civilization.

**Sharp Perception** Kemono have sharp senses. Whenever you test Perception (even without the skill or Sense), the corresponding attribute has +1.

**Talent** Kemono have certain physical or mental aptitudes, depending on their animal heritage. Choose an advantage from Agile, Charismatic, Strong, Genius, Resolute, or Vigorous.

**Twitch** Kemono demonstrate some negative trait linked to their animal side: rabbits can be cautious, foxes treacherous, and so on. Choose a disadvantage from Unfriendly, Clumsy, Weak, Fragile, Indecisive, or Clumsy.

#### Medusa (1pt)
Medusas are a race with humanoid bodies and hair made of serpents. Some have exotic colored eyes with vertical pupils, while others even have scaly skin and/or a forked tongue.

Despite the legends about monstrous and reclusive medusas capable of petrifying victims, those who came as Travelers seem to belong to a different variety. They are civilized, sociable, communicative, and somewhat vain. They love social media, becoming content producers, influencers, and streamers. In fact, they use and frequent the networks so naturally that it makes many wonder; is there something similar in the afterlife Convergence?!

Not all of them live off surfing, of course — or rather, they don't live off it alone. Many end up as Arcanauts, menestreamers, broadcasting their adventures online to legions of followers.

**Charismatic** Medusas have an easy time influencing people.

**Stunning Gaze** You can spend one movement and 3MP to force a nearby creature to make a Resistance test, opposed to its Power.If you fail, for one round the target cannot take actions, and suffers Loss on defense tests.

**Weakling** Medusas should get off the hypernet and exercise more.

#### Minotaur (1pt)
Minotaurs are very robust humanoids with bovine heads. Although the bull head is the most common, there are those with the heads of buffalo, wildebeest, bison, musk ox, elk and other related animals. Some have split hooves instead of feet. The typical minotaur carries with it the feeling of belonging to something greater, more glorious, but lost forever. Perhaps for this reason, almost all are proud, serious and determined. Very competitive, it is common for them to resolve disagreements with physical dispute, which can be an arm wrestling match or simply a good fight. The most successful end up as sports stars, while others become security guards, soldiers or adventurers.

Females are called minauras, but their relationship with minotaurs is unclear; they seem to have an instinctive aversion to each other, for no known reason. Another repressed memory from beyond the Convergence? Whatever the reason, on Earth, both end up seeking partners from other peoples.

**Athletic** Minotaurs are adept at physical activities. Whenever you test Athletics (even if you don’t have the skill), the corresponding attribute has +1.

**Labyrinth Sense** You always know your way around any place you’ve been. In addition, you always have Gain on Perception checks to sniff out.

**Derangement (Phobia)** Minotaurs are afraid of heights. Being near a fall of 10 feet or more activates this disadvantage.

#### Ogre (1pt)
Among the beings beyond the Convergence, there are huge and brutal humanoids. The most savage inhabit the Arks, wearing furs and using clubs, but others live in cities or become Arcanauts. Although they belong to different types, they are collectively known as ogres — “ogre” has become a synonym for any huge non-human (and even some humans).
Although they are sought after for their strength, acting as security guards, soldiers and mercenaries, some become experts in unexpected areas, such as engineering hyperspace portals.

**Destroyer** Almost nothing causes more damage than an ogre. In violent combat, when you score a critical hit while attacking a Nearby opponent, you can pay 2MP to add your Power once more. You can only
do this once per attack, even if you score more than one critical hit.

**Intimidator** Almost nothing is as scary as an ogre. You always have Gain on Influence checks to scare, intimidate, or
frighten someone.

**Different** Almost nothing is as big as an ogre.

#### Osteon 2pt
Many Arks are infested with mindless skeleton soldiers, animated by necromantic magic or negative energy, created as minions of evil spellcasters or appearing spontaneously in cursed areas.

However, not all are walking, fighting piles of bones. Skeletons with intelligence and a will of their own are called osteons. Unlike those that crawl in hordes on the Arks, they demonstrate the same consciousness and civility as other peoples. Even a little more.

It is not known what causes an osteon to appear. Many are travelers, but there are cases of deceased people who return as osteons right here on Earth. In any case, they still seek their place in this world, whether as tireless workers, soldiers in special forces, obsessive researchers, or even Arcanauts.

**Immune (Abiotic, Diseases, Resilient).** So, you are undead.

**Afterlife.** Choose any skill. Whenever you test it (even if you don’t have the skill), the corresponding attribute has +1.

**Lifeless** That’s right, you are still undead.

#### Qareen (2pt)
Qareen are magical beings. Not only because of their magnificent appearance, but also because they descend from mortals and arcane creatures, such as genies, nymphs, fairies and others. For this reason they are also known as half-genies.
Qareen are born with elaborate tattoos, which glow when they use their magical powers. No one knows what they mean, but their design is very reminiscent of the glyphs at the entrance to the Arks.

Generous, helpful and always eager to help, qareen like to live among other people, lending them their great arcane power.
In fact, their magic is strongest when used to grant the wishes of others.

**Wishes** You have the Magic advantage. In addition, when you cast a spell that someone else has requested since your last turn, the cost decreases by –2MP (minimum 1MP). Making a wish to the qareen does not cost any actions or movements (but the qareen still needs actions or movements to cast the spell).

**Charismatic** Qareen have an easy time influencing people.

**Code of Gratitude** Every qareen will be grateful to someone who has done them a great favor, adopting that person as their “master” until the debt of gratitude is equalized. A qareen can have more than one master at a time — and yes, that will be a problem.

#### Sauroid (2pt)
Sauroids are a variety of reptilian beings, combining human traits with lizards, crocodiles, snakes, dinosaurs, and others. In addition to the reptilian head, they can have scaly bodies, tails, crests, claws, and shells.
In general, sauroids are more robust and rustic than kemono — but, like the latter, they are fully capable of adopting any modern career and living in society. They can be seen in cities leading ordinary lives, with jobs and bills to pay. Due to their physical abilities, however, many become soldiers (whether in security forces or villain armies), as well as adventurers or Arcanauts.

Some say that sauroids are evolved dinosaurs. Others suspect that they were created by some reptilian god from beyond the Convergence. And for the conspiracy theorists, reptilians have always existed on Earth, and now they can prove it! 

**Shelled** You gain Resolute and Vigorous.

**Camouflage** Sauroids have careful movements, coloration that matches their surroundings, and a lot of patience. You always have Gain on tests to hide.

**Weakness (Cold)** You are slow in the cold. Wear a light jacket.

#### Vampire (1pt)
Gallant and seductive, vampires are among the most powerful beings in the Age of Arks. But, as we know, they pay dearly for this power. Vampires can be undead or not. They can arise in many ways, magical or scientific. Ancient curse, demonic ritual, rare disease, being created by another vampire, or even belonging to some extraterrestrial people.
Vampires are immortal, but they need to suck the life out of the living. Those
who do not want to kill find other means: they drink from animals, resort to synthetic substitutes, access (or steal from) blood banks, and find volunteer donors.

So, not only is it possible to be a non-murderous vampire, but there are some publicly known ones, in prominent positions in society. Many others become adventurers, in search of a cure for their condition or the wealth necessary to survive.

**Talent** Vampires have superior aptitudes. Choose an advantage from Agile, Charismatic, Strong, Genius, Resolute, or Vigorous.

**Immortal** You’re a root vampire, not a whiny goth vampire — that’s a whole other ballgame. Even after you turn to ash, you come back.

**Weakness (daylight)** You kind of don’t like the sun.

**Dependency** You don’t have this disadvantage, because you found a way to overcome it. It’s only here for NPC villains. No, the total cost of the archetype doesn’t change, you’re just not greedy anymore.

# Playing the game (page 104)

**3 Dice…** Yes, you must have seen those weird RPG dice sets around. Maybe you even have some. But for this game, you won’t need them. 3DeT Victory uses just the good old six-sided die. Dice is abbreviated as D. Whenever a number appears before D, that’s how many dice you’ll roll, and then add up the results. 3D means roll three dice and add them all up. If you roll three dice and get 2, 4, and 3, the result is 9. Easy, right?

Whenever a number appears after D, it will also have a plus (+) or minus (-) sign. You roll the dice and then add or subtract that number from the result. 3D+4 means roll three dice and then add 4 to the total. 2D-1 means roll two dice and subtract 1 from the total.
You’ll be rolling dice to make tests. In the game world, your character will attempt difficult or dangerous things: decipher a secret code, recognize a disguise, jump over a chasm, dodge a sword, attack a monster. The tests tell you whether these actions were successful or not. In any test you will roll 1D, 2D or 3D. You will always roll at least one die, and at most three. The more, the better. The rules explained below tell you how many dice you will roll in each test. As a player, your goal is to roll as many dice as you can, to try to get the highest possible total. The player must go for all three dice. To help you remember, think of the name of the game: 3DeT Victory. Three Dice and Test. Brilliant, right? (What? Did you hear it was “Tokyo Defenders”? I don’t know what you’re talking about!) You only need one die to play. Roll as many times as you need and add up the results. But the more dice, the better. Ideally, each player should have his or her own set of three dice.

**…and Test:** Spot a ninja approaching. Hit the back of the cannibal giant’s head. Get noticed by senpai. Whenever the character tries something dangerous, reckless, or dramatic, the GM can (will!) require a test. A test is always based on one of three attributes: Power, Skill, or Endurance.

The choice of the attribute tested depends on the situation. Impressing the audience with your music will be a Power test. Maneuvering the flying motorcycle will be a Skill test. Resisting the mental domination of the alien sorceress will be a Resistance test. This choice is made by the GM.

When making a test, the player rolls the correct amount of dice (1, 2 or 3) and adds the chosen attribute. So, when making a Power test, a character with P3 rolls the dice and adds 3 to the result. Test = Dice + Attribute. To be successful, the result of the test must reach the goal. This goal is a target number chosen by the GM, according to the difficulty of the task. If the test is equal to or greater than the goal, the test is a success. If it is less than the goal, the test fails.

How many dice should you roll, anyway?
**1D.** This die is the base. In any test, you will roll at least one die.
**+1D.** You can roll one more die if you have a skill appropriate to the situation.
**+1D.** You can roll one more die if you have a Gain. There are advantages and situations that offer Gain.
**–1D.** You will roll one less die (at least 1) if you have a Loss.

A Gain cancels out a Loss, and vice versa. Your goal as a player is to reach three dice, as many as possible. Therefore, the best test involves having the right skill and also having Gain.

Let's say the character is going to attack a monster. You will roll 1 die. If you have the Fighting skill, you will roll 2 dice. If there is also an advantageous situation for you (for example, if you have a Fighter Helper and spend MP), then you will roll all 3 dice. (And in either case, it will add your Power value to the total result.)

**Non-skill tests.** The vast majority of 3DeT Victory tests have an associated skill. Sometimes, however, you may need to roll a roll for which no skill seems appropriate. If there is no skill in the game that matches that test, then the die is granted for free — that is, you make the normal test with 2D. Note that this is different from a skill test that exists, but the player does not have it! In this case, he will roll only one die. You can also receive an extra die for a Win, or roll only 1D if you have a Loss.

The **Goal** is a target number chosen by the GM, according to the difficulty of the task: 
- 6 (Easy) Open a jammed jar. Drive a well-tuned car on an empty road. Wake up to the sound of the alarm clock and get up. Easy tasks are almost automatic, often the GM does not even need to ask for this test
- 9 (Average) Break down a locked door. Drive a crappy car in busy traffic. Finish a spicy dish. 
- 12 (Hard) Break down a fortified wall. Ride a motorcycle in a world championship. Survive the venomous bite of a Komodo dragon.
- 15 (Very Hard) Pry open the jaws of a kaiju. Pilot a fighter jet through an invading fleet. Say “no” to a god’s plea. 

Whenever a number appears in parentheses after an attribute or skill—for example, Power (9) or Lore (12)—that is the goal. Other times, the goal might be the sum of a fixed value and an attribute of the target. An Art test (6 + target Power) against a target with P4, for example, would have a target of 10. The GM chooses the target he or she deems appropriate for each task, as shown in the examples. Note that increasing or decreasing the target is not the same as having a Gain/Loss. Targets do not change the number of dice in the test; Gains and Losses do. You will discover later that this makes a big difference. For average tests, a character without the right skill will have difficulty, but a trained character with good attributes will succeed most of the time. On difficult and very difficult tests, even a trained character will need high attributes, or luck, or both, to succeed.

**Doubling the goal** By accumulating critical rolls, you can exceed the goal of the roll by a significant amount. Not only will you be able to accomplish the task requested, but you will do it almost perfectly! Whenever the roll adds up to double or more of the requested goal, you will achieve a much better result than you expected. Usually it is just a descriptive detail; you can say exactly how you managed to accomplish what you wanted. Sometimes, however, the GM may reward you with a Gain on a later roll that is related to the result of this one.

**Resisted goal** Sometimes, the goal will be another character's roll. If you are trying to deceive someone with a disguise, the goal of your Streetwise roll will be the result of the target's Perception roll. In these cases, the active character (the one taking the initiative) has the advantage. He must equal or surpass the passive character's roll. In other words, in the event of a tie, the active character wins. If there is no side more active than the other (such as two characters arm wrestling), in a tie, the one with the highest attribute wins. If there is still a tie, both sides roll one die each until the tie is broken.

## Gain and Loss
It is much easier to hit an opponent who has not yet noticed your presence. It will be easier to dodge shots if there is cover to take cover. Running away from the dragon will be difficult if you are running through mud.

**Gain** is when a situation is in your favor, making a task easier. In this case, you can roll one more die. Loss is when a situation is against you, making the task harder. In this case, you roll one less die (at least 1). Gains and Losses affecting the same test cancel each other out. You do not roll any more or less dice. Gains and Losses are not cumulative. You can never gain (or lose) more than one die due to Gains and Losses, even if they come from different sources. However, if you have more than one Gain, only one of them is canceled out by a Loss; in this case, you can still roll an extra die. The opposite also occurs: if you have two Losses, only one will be canceled out by a Gain. Gains • Positive situation. Taking cover in a firefight. Attacking from a higher place. Making a sharp turn in a race. Convince
someone who owes you a debt.

- Help. Companions can help you accomplish a task. An ally with the same skill, using an action to help you, grants a
Gain. Without the skill, you need to gather three or more allies to grant a Gain. On a difficult test (12 or more), only help from characters with the skill grants a Gain.
- Advantages. Some advantages offer favorable conditions on certain tests, and/or for a cost in MP. If you have a Helper (Fighter), you can spend 2MP to gain a Gain on an attack test.
- Style. Doing things in original, crazy, and irresponsible ways is part of 3DeT Victory. A Gain can be obtained by doing something very brave, or very foolish, or very cool! If the GM judges that your action is stylish, even if it is difficult (such as crossing a boiling lake while performing a ballet step), he may decide that the condition is favorable. This only works the first time; don’t expect to gain extra dice by repeating the same idea over and over again!
- Dharma. When you really need a Gain, but have no way of getting it, you can resort to dharma—a reward from the universe to the righteous and virtuous. Tell the GM that you had a sudden inspiration, a flash of luck, a memory of past lessons, or anything else without further justification. But be careful: after doing this, the balance of the universe will punish you with its opposite, karma, when you least expect it (that is, when the GM wants it to). 

After using dharma once, you can only resort to it again after suffering the corresponding karma.

**Loss**
- Negative situation. Fighting without seeing the opponent. Moving on rough terrain. Operating unknown alien equipment. Acting under some interference or distraction. Being defeated (see below).
- Advantages. Some advantages (such as Irresistible), instead of putting the character in a Gain, leave the opponent in a Loss.
- Disadvantages. Some disadvantages cause Loss in certain situations. A Monstrous character has Loss to make others trust him.
- Karma. You are not the only one who can call on the justice of the universe to get out of trouble. Sometimes, the villain only needs a resentful speech — or even a look of pure hatred — for you to feel the evil cosmo-energy taking a die off your roll. The good news is that, after suffering this setback, you gain a use of dharma to use whenever you want. Use it wisely!

## Action Points
Anime heroes seem to be ready for anything, with the right solution to any problem, or they just have incredible luck. RPG players, however, can rarely boast the same thing! Action Points exist to fulfill this role, to give players some control over chance and destiny.

**Action Points (AP)** represent a mix of adrenaline, good fortune, and tricks up your sleeve. When you use AP, it’s as if the incredible luck of anime protagonists comes to your rescue. You spend points to turn a failure into a success, or a success into an even greater success.

**You can spend 1 Action Point to make a Gain or cancel a Loss on a test.**

But be careful! Just like incredible luck, Action Points run out quickly! If you use them too early, or too quickly, you may end up with none for when you really need them.

## Critical Hit
When you make a test, whenever you get a result of 6, you add the attribute tested once more. A result of 6 is a critical hit.

For example, an attacker with Power 4 rolls two dice, getting a 6 and a 2. He has scored a critical hit. His attack will be 8 (P4+P4) +6 for the roll of the first die, +2 for the second die, for a total attack of 16. 

When rolling 2 or 3 dice, it is possible to get more than one 6 — therefore, more than one critical hit. **Each critical hit adds the attribute once more.**

Example: A super spy trying to go unnoticed makes a Skill test. He has H4. He also has the Streetwise skill (so he rolls two dice) and the Invisible advantage (he has Gain, so he rolls one more die). When he makes his roll, he gets three 6s. A devastating hit! In addition to his 18 on the roll, he adds his H4 four times, for a final total of 34. Even a god would have trouble noticing him!

Increasing the critical chance is possible. Some advantages improve your chance of getting critical hits. Instead of a 6, a 5 will also be a critical.

If there is more than one power that improves the critical chance, they stack. A Charismatic character testing a skill with which he has Mastery, for example, gets a critical on a 4, 5 or 6 on the die.

A critical on a 4, 5 or 6 (that is, a 50% chance) is the upper limit. Even if you have more than two powers that increase critical hits, a roll of 3 or lower is always a normal result, not a critical hit.

Some disadvantages (such as Weak or Fragile), on the other hand, cancel out your critical chance. Powers that improve critical chance no longer apply; the chance is not reduced, it is canceled out.

There are also ways to add more times to a critical hit, such as a Power Attack. Remember that the attribute is always added more times, not multiplied. A critical hit with a Power Special Attack adds the Power three times, not six! These powers can only be used once per roll, unless their description says otherwise. Finally, some advantages, techniques, and situations (such as power scaling) can also provide automatic critical hits. When this happens, you add the attribute one more time automatically, before rolling the dice. You can still get more critical hits with the roll, achieving devastating results!

A critical failure is what happens when all the dice in the roll (no matter how many) result in a 1. In addition to failing the test completely, something bad will happen. Almost always, something more funny than harmful. The most common is that you made a mistake (you tripped, you dropped your weapon, your helmet covered your vision...).

As a general rule, a critical failure will put you at a disadvantage: you are down, off balance, inattentive... So your next test will be Loss. The GM may choose other effects for the failure. Note that the fewer dice you roll, the greater your chance of having a critical failure!

## Combat Round
When two or more characters are at odds with each other, try to cross the same bridge, or reach the last slice of pizza, a conflict begins! Combats and other conflicts follow the following steps:
- Step 1 — Initiative. Everyone makes a Skill test, using the Fighting skill (or another skill appropriate for that type of conflict). The characters take their turns in order from highest result to lowest. In the event of a tie, the one with the highest Skill acts first. If there is a tie again, both roll a second time. Initiative is rolled only once,
at the beginning of combat; the order of turns is maintained throughout the confrontation.

- Step 2 — Attack. The characters choose their targets and make their attacks or maneuvers, each on their own initiative. To make a simple attack, the attacker makes a Power test,
using the Fighting skill (or another skill appropriate for that type of conflict). The goal of the attack is the defense of the target.

- Step 3 — Defense. The defender makes a Resistance test, with the Fighting skill (or another skill of his own). This is his defense. The damage is equal to the difference between the attack and defense, reducing the target's HP. Every attack causes at least 1 point of damage, even if the defense is equal or greater, except in a perfect defense: when the defense test doubles the attack test (if the attacker rolls 4 and the target rolls 8 or more, for example). In this case the target takes no damage. Some powers and situations (such as a higher scale) can make all defenses perfect.

## Turn, Round, Scene, Session (page 120)
3DeT Victory is a storytelling game. In the stories we tell, and in our imaginations, time passes as we want it to. You say
“two years have passed” and voila — timeskip, two years have passed! You can, and will, use real-life time measurements — hours, days, years. But the most important units of time are four:
- A turn is the shortest and most widely used measurement of time. A turn is your turn to play; each player has his turn, his turn (including the GM). A turn is not an exact measurement in real time. It can be a few
seconds (in combat) or up to a minute (in calmer scenes).
- A round is the sum of all the players’ turns. A round begins when the first character acts, and ends after the last one has acted (who acts first is determined by initiative, seen in Combat Round). Certain events in the game, such as the duration of some effects or spells, are measured in rounds.
- A scene is a situation, an event with a beginning, middle, and end—think of a scene as a set of things that happen in the same place at the same time. A fight is a scene. A conversation between two or more characters is a scene. An uneventful journey through the mountains is a scene (if there are incidents, each is a scene of its own). A scene can last a few minutes, or hours, days, months. The GM decides when a scene begins or ends.
- A session is the amount of time spent playing in real life, regardless of how much time passes in the game world. It begins when everyone gathers to play, and ends when everyone gets up from the table or turns off their webcam. A typical session lasts between one and three hours or more. Some effects in the game are limited (or required) per session.

## Action and Movement
On your turn, each character can perform one action and one movement.
Action is almost always the most important thing you can do on your turn. Making an attack, using a technique, and casting a spell are all actions.
Using an advantage that costs MP is not an action, except when it is part of an attack (such as a Special Attack).
Not everything a character does has to be an action. Talking to allies during combat, to discuss strategy and give instructions, can be done freely while you perform other actions.

The +Members advantage gives you an extra action on your turn. In this case, you can act twice and move once, or give up one or two actions in exchange for movement.

Movement is almost always your ability to move.
Each character can move a certain distance with one movement (see Distances below).
On your turn, if you wish, you can make two movements and no actions. But you cannot do the opposite (stand still and take two actions).

Certain things can be done as if they were moves: throwing something for an ally to pick up, opening or closing a door, getting in or out of a vehicle. Some advantages and powers tell you whether you need actions or moves to use them. When nothing is said, then it is an action, unless the GM says otherwise.

The Haste advantage gives you an extra move on your turn.

In this case, you can move twice and act, or forgo your action and move three times.

A **full action** is a more complex or time-consuming action.
You must use your entire turn to perform it, expending both the action and the movement. You cannot take extra actions or movements when you perform a full action.
A **reaction** is an action that occurs in response to another character’s action, or another external event. A reaction does not occur on your own turn—it occurs on the turn of the action or event that caused it. For example, using a Special Defense against an enemy’s attack is a reaction.

## Ranges (page 122)

3DeT Victory isn’t about tactical combat, but rather fast and furious fighting. Still, the distance and positioning of characters does affect your options. In game terms, there are four ranges: Close, Far, Very Far, and Out of Range.

**Close** is when something can be reached with a simple, melee attack. This is the range for punches, kicks, and sword strikes.

**Far Away** is when something cannot be reached with melee attacks. You can only attack with shots or projectiles (or by stretching?); the Range (1pt) advantage represents this type of ranged attack. Without it, a character attacking something Far Away has a Loss.

**Very Far Away** is when something is much further away. You can attack only with the Range (1pt) advantage and still have a Loss. You can attack without Loss if you have the better version of Range (2pt).

**Out of Range** is when something is still in sight, but too far away to be attacked. Only some spells and powers (such as Telepath) can reach it. You can use a move to change a distance between Near/Far and Far/Very Far. For Very Far/Out of Range, you need to use two moves (i.e., a full round).

But HOW CLOSE is it? As already mentioned, 3DeT Victory is not a wargame with maps and miniatures and rulers. It is about opponents separated by a few steps, who then jump two hundred meters — with kinetic visual effects in the background — to reach the now distant opponent with a special strike!

When we say Near and Far, this is what we mean: close and far. Close being what you can touch with your hands or weapons (without stretching!) and Far being what you can't.

If you have trouble imagining such distances, consider these simple measurements:

- Close. Up to 1m
- Far. Between 1 and 10m
- Very Far. Between 10 and 100m
- Out of Range. More than 100m

These measurements are not exact, they are just approximations. A large scythe, of course, has a range of more than 1m — but it does not reach Far. A thrown stuffed rabbit does not suddenly lose all its strength and fall when the opponent is 11m away. Its accusing voice, perfectly audible up to 100m away, does not completely stop at 101m. Just use your intuition and common sense. Inside a cavernous, cramped chamber in an Ark, nothing will be Too Far. In an elevator, nothing will be Far. Attacking targets on the top of a building, nothing will be Near or Far.
